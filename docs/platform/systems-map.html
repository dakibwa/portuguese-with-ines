Português com a Inês, becoming a platform for any tutor · read from the code on main, 9 November 2026

How the tutor platform fits together

Every system hangs off two records, the student and the lesson. That core is simple, and it is the right shape for many tutors. What stops a tutor working their own way is eight rules written into the code instead of the tutor's settings, and one hole: students without an email never hear from the site.

Simple? Yes, at the core

Two records, one settings table, one money module, one clock. Weekly runs are ordinary lessons, so nothing special-cases them.

Intuitive? For the tutor, mostly

One card and one next button per student. Money states leak into the words she reads 10. For students it is unproven: 3 of 21 have ever signed in.

Room to work their way? Not yet

Notice, fees, no-shows, trials, places, lesson lengths and homework language are fixed in code. All fixable, mostly small.

Arrows show what one system does to another today. The numbers mark the spots where a tutor can't do it their own way, or where the system is harder than it needs to be. Each number is explained further down.

At any step the tutor can step outside the rules by hand: her own moves skip the student's notice rule, she can add a lesson outside her hours, tick Already paid, or make a lesson free. That escape hatch is a big part of why the system already suits Inês.

The change that opens up most of the rules

A lesson today remembers only the name of the wording its student agreed to. Every move or cancel is judged against the tutor's settings as they are now. So if a tutor raised her notice from 14 to 24 hours, students who booked under 14 would suddenly be charged under 24. To stop that, the settings screen refuses anything above 14 hours or €5. Copying the terms onto each lesson when it is booked removes the reason for the cap.

Where a tutor can't do it their way yet

Ordered by how much each one would stop a new tutor. The first nine block something a tutor will reasonably want; the last two make the system harder than it needs to be.

1
Booking

Notice and late fee can't go above 14 hours and €5

Today
The change rule reads the tutor's current settings for every lesson, so raising them would reach lessons already booked. The settings refuse more than 14 hours or €5 to prevent that.
Fix
Copy notice, late fee and no-show rule onto each lesson and weekly run at booking, as drawn above. Then open the limits to 0 to 72 hours and up to the lesson price.

policy.mjs changePolicy · index.mjs /admin/settings ranges

2
Payments

A no-show always costs the late fee

Today
A no-show is charged the late-change fee (€5) instead of the lesson. Many tutors charge the full lesson when a student doesn't turn up.
Fix
Its own setting: full price, a fixed fee, or nothing.

index.mjs lessonChargePlan

3
Messages

Reminders stop if notice is 24 hours or more

Today
The reminder goes out between the notice point and 24 hours before the lesson. Set the notice to 24 hours and that window is empty, so no reminder is ever sent and nothing says so.
Fix
Time the reminder from the tutor's own notice, for example 10 hours before free changes end, or give it its own setting.

index.mjs sendLessonReminders, REMINDER_LEAD_HOURS = 24

4
Messages

Students with no email hear nothing from the site

Today
Every automatic message is an email, and the sender skips students with no address. That was 11 of Inês's 21 students on 2 November: no confirmation, reminder or pay link. WhatsApp exists only as links she taps herself.
Fix
Choose the channel per student. Email where there is one; otherwise the message waits in the tutor's Needs you as a ready WhatsApp link. WhatsApp's business API can come later.

email.mjs send, skips "no-email-yet"

5
Student

A student's own price exists only for 60 and 90 minutes

Today
The database allows a student's own price only for 60 or 90 minutes, and 90 is 1.5 times the hour unless set. A tutor teaching 30 or 45 minutes can't give a student their own price.
Fix
Keep a student's price per lesson type, any length, with "work it out from the hour" as an option.

schema.sql student_recurring_rates · rates.mjs withDerived

6
Booking

"Trial" is a fixed lesson, not a setting

Today
One per student, can't repeat weekly, keeps its own price: all of it hangs on the id trial, checked in 11 places.
Fix
Three switches on every lesson type: first lesson only, can repeat weekly, student's own price applies. Then a tutor can offer a free 20-minute intro, a paid trial, or neither.

index.mjs, rates.mjs lessonPriceSnapshotSql

7
Lesson

In person means Porto

Today
A lesson's place must be online or porto, by a database rule on lessons and weekly runs.
Fix
A list of places per tutor (online, a city, the student's home, a café), each lesson pointing at one. The plan has this; it needs a migration, not only a new label.

schema.sql bookings.location CHECK

8
Homework

Homework is written in European Portuguese only

Today
The AI is told to use "Portuguese as spoken and written in Portugal". Levels are fixed at A1 to C2 and length at 10, 15 or 20 minutes.
Fix
Language and variant from the tutor record (Brazilian Portuguese, Mexican Spanish), levels from the tutor's curriculum, length as a setting.

homework.mjs draft instructions

9
Student

A student has no level of their own

Today
The dashboard shows the level of the latest homework sheet. With no homework there is no level, and the curriculum will need one.
Fix
Store the level on the student, with the date it changed. Homework starts from it and the curriculum moves it.

StudentDashboard.tsx · homework.mjs lastLevel

10
Payments · complexity

Nine payment states, plus one hidden in another column

Today
A lesson's payment can be in nine states, and "paid by hand" is stored in the column meant for consent wording. The late fee has five states of its own. The pages have to turn this into words like "Lessons not on card".
Fix
Two plain questions per lesson: how is it paid (card, by hand, free, record only) and where is it (to come, due, paid, failed, refunded). Before many tutors arrive, with npm run test:booking, since it is money.

policy.mjs PAID_BY_HAND_MARK · bookings.payment_status

11
Platform · complexity

Three ways in have no web address

Today
Build step 2 picks the tutor from the web address. Three entry points carry none: the every-minute clock, Stripe's webhook and the return from connecting Google Calendar.
Fix
The clock walks a list of tutors (only those with work due), the Stripe event names its connected account, and the Google sign-in state names the tutor.

index.mjs scheduled(), /stripe/webhook, /google-calendar/callback

Already simple, worth keeping as it is

  • Two records carry everything. A weekly run is a real lesson each week, so payments, homework and the calendar never need a special case for it.
  • The rules sit in one settings table, read by one loader. That is exactly the shape of one tutor record.
  • Money runs in one Stripe module on one clock, so connecting each tutor's own Stripe account is a contained change.
  • The tutor can always step outside the rules: move without notice, add a lesson outside her hours, tick Already paid, make a lesson free.
  • Homework hangs off one lesson, one sheet each, and the tutor's lesson notes stay private.
  • Students without an email are still full students. Inês can book, price and charge them; only messages miss them 4.

The curriculum: a plan and homework for every lesson

Before each lesson it drafts a plan for that student. After it, it drafts homework that practises what was taught and brings back what they got wrong. Both follow what language-learning research has found works, and both are built for the language being taught. The tutor stays in charge: as with homework today, nothing reaches the student until she has read it and approved it.

What the research says, and what the curriculum does with it

What worksResearchWhat the curriculum does
A balance of four kinds of work: understanding, speaking or writing, studying the language, and building fluencyNation, the four strands (2007)Checks that each week of lessons and homework gives each about a quarter of the time, so it never becomes grammar drills alone
Recalling from memory teaches more than rereadingRoediger and Karpicke, the testing effect (2006)Homework asks students to produce answers, never just to reread notes
Review spread over days and weeks lasts longer than review crammed togetherCepeda and colleagues, spacing meta-analysis (2006)Each sheet brings back items from earlier lessons at growing gaps, sooner for the ones marked wrong
Lessons built around real tasks, with grammar brought in as the task needs itEllis, task-based teaching (2003); CEFR can-do descriptors (Council of Europe, 2020)Each plan's goal is a can-do, such as "can arrange to meet a friend by phone", not a grammar heading
Short, explicit teaching of a troublesome form helpsNorris and Ortega, meta-analysis (2000)A brief, direct explanation of the form the student keeps getting wrong, then practice in use
The most frequent words give the biggest returnNation, Learning Vocabulary in Another Language (2013)New words come from the language's frequency bands for the level, plus words for the student's own goals
Prompting learners to correct themselves leads to more repair than handing them the right formLyster and Ranta, corrective feedback (1997)Marks group a student's errors into patterns, so the tutor knows what to prompt for next lesson

Built for each language

  • The path follows the language's own guide to the CEFR levels: the Referencial Camões and CAPLE exams for Portuguese, the Instituto Cervantes Plan curricular and DELE for Spanish, DELF for French, Profile Deutsch and the Goethe exams for German. Other languages use their own scale, such as HSK for Mandarin and JLPT for Japanese.
  • The variant is the tutor's choice: European or Brazilian Portuguese, Spain or Latin American Spanish.
  • Words come from frequency lists for that language.
  • Trouble spots depend on the student's first language. An English speaker learning Portuguese needs ser and estar, nasal vowels, and false friends like puxar, which means pull. A Spanish speaker already knows much of the grammar and needs pronunciation and false friends instead.
  • Pace follows how long the language takes. The US Foreign Service Institute puts Portuguese or Spanish at about 24 to 30 weeks of full-time study for an English speaker, and Japanese or Mandarin at about 88.

Who makes each language pack

AI drafts a pack once per language and variant from those references. A native-speaker teacher checks it before any tutor gets it; Inês would check the European Portuguese one first. Each tutor can then edit their copy, add their own materials, or switch the curriculum off.

What the data needs

The language pack; the student's level, goals and first language 9; a plan on each lesson and the topics it covered; and a review list fed by the marks. Inês's free-text plan for each student becomes their goals.

The research backs these principles across languages but doesn't crown one method, and most of it comes from classrooms rather than one-to-one lessons. That is another reason the tutor approves every plan instead of the curriculum deciding.
