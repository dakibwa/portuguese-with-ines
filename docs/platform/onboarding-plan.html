Tutor onboarding A plan for the platform, 9 November 2026. Chapeau is still the working name. No calls and no waiting on a person: every step is self-serve. Every sketch shows a sample tutor, Maria, who teaches Spanish online and in Valencia.

From hello to a live site in twenty minutes.

A tutor arrives with a marketplace profile, a photo and students they already teach. Every screen asks one question, and every answer shows on their own site right beside it. About twenty minutes in, the site is live on a free address. Getting paid and inviting students take about twenty more, straight away or another day.

How it should feel Six rules to check every screen against.

How it should feel

Their site from the first answer

The site is drawn beside the questions from the start. Type Maria and the heading reads Spanish with Maria. There's no big reveal at the end, because nothing is hidden along the way.

One question per screen

Each screen asks one thing in plain words, with a good answer already filled in, so most screens end with reading and pressing Next. Every screen works one-handed on a phone, and every answer saves as it's given.

Nothing starts blank

Pages are drafted from what they paste, prices from the rate they charge now, rules from common choices. The drafting never adds a qualification, a number of years or a review the tutor didn't give.

Live before it's finished

The site goes live once it has a name, a photo, a lesson and some hours. Stripe, students and a domain come after, from the dashboard. Until Stripe is connected, students book and pay the tutor directly.

Terms in plain sentences

Tutors set their rules by filling in the sentences their students will read: book at least 24 hours ahead. The site enforces exactly what the sentence says.

Quiet, with three celebrations

No confetti, no percentage bars, no sparkle icons and no thinking animations. The hat tips three times: when the site goes live, at the first booking, and when the first card payment arrives.

The whole flow Times are targets to test with the first tutors, not measurements.

Twelve steps, one branch

Steps 1 to 9 happen in one sitting and end with the site live. Steps 10 and 11 then wait on the dashboard and can be done in either order, that day or later. Only step 10 branches.

Your photo

Your story

5 min
What it asksPaste anything they've written about their teaching: a Preply or italki profile, a bio, an old flyer. With nothing to paste, three short questions: who they teach, what a lesson is like, what students say.
Already filled inHome, About, Lessons and Questions pages, drafted in their voice and in the site's language. Drafted lines stay highlighted until the tutor reads or changes them.
How it should feelA friend who writes well tidied their notes. Tap any line to keep it, rewrite it or remove it.
The drafting ruleFacts come only from their own words. A missing detail becomes a gap, such as years teaching, to fill in or delete. Nothing is invented to make a page look fuller.

How it should feelTrying on, rather than browsing a catalogue of other people's sites. A line under the colours says they can change them any time, free.

Lessons and prices

2 min

Already filled in30, 60 and 90 minutes. Prices from the rate in their pasted profile, saying where it came from (from your italki rate of €22), or else a middle price for their language. The currency follows their country.
How it should feelA price list on a blackboard. Three lines, and done.

Your week

2 min
What it asksShade the hours they teach on a week grid, or tap a starting point: weekday evenings, mornings or weekends.
Already filled inTheir time zone, in words (Valencia time). One button connects Google Calendar so lessons arrive with a Meet link, as on Inês's site. It can wait for later.

8

Your rules

2 min
What it asksOne short paragraph with blanks: how far ahead and how late students can book, the gap after each lesson, the fee for a late change and what a missed lesson costs.
Already filled inA common choice in every blank, with a note that says so.
How it should feelWriting house rules on one card. The preview turns to their terms page, which is written from these sentences.
Why it's safeThe terms page, the booking screens and the emails all read these numbers. A change applies to new bookings only, because each booking keeps the terms its student agreed to.

Your address, then live

2 min
What it asksAn address, such as maria.chapeau.school, checked as they type. Then a read-through of at most five things worth a look, each fixed in place. Then one button: Put my site live.
Already filled inAn address made from their name. Their own domain can come any time later, and the free address keeps working.

10

Get paid

10 min
What it asksOne paragraph to read, then Stripe's own form shown inside the page in the platform's colours and type: ID and a bank account, once. Then how students pay: card charged when the lesson ends, by hand, or both.
Already filled inName, email, country and site address, passed to Stripe so it asks less. Card after the lesson is picked to start with.
How it should feelThe bank part is handled by a bank, and the paragraph says so plainly: the money goes straight to them and never passes through us.
The branchWhere Stripe doesn't serve the tutor's country, this card says their students pay them directly, and the site switches to paid by hand on the same plan. It reads as a choice, never as an error.
11

Bring your students

10 min
What it asksPaste a list as it comes (names with phone numbers or emails, in any order), upload a spreadsheet, or add one person. For each, if they like: a usual weekly time and a personal price.
Already filled inColumns sorted out from the paste, and phone numbers put in international form from the tutor's country. A usual time sets up that student's weekly lessons.
How it should feelSending a message they'd have sent anyway. Each student gets the site's WhatsApp invite in the tutor's voice, one tap each.
Marketplace studentsPreply's rules forbid moving students off Preply through Preply's own messages. One line on this screen says so and suggests inviting only students they already reach by phone or email.

The first booking

Milestone
What it asksNothing. This one happens to them.
Already filled inThe dashboard shows their week. The setup card lists what's left and goes away once it's all done.
How it should feelThe second hat tip: Chapeau! Ana booked her first lesson. The third comes with the first card payment.
If they stop partwayOne email the next day says what's left, with a link back to the same screen. It's sent once and never repeated as a series of reminders.
Left out on purpose Each has a default that works, so none of it stands between a tutor and a first booking.

Not in onboarding

  • Their own domainSettings, any time. The free address keeps working and points to it.
  • An intro videoTheir About page, added from the dashboard.
  • More lesson types and placesLessons in Settings. Personal prices sit on each student.
  • Email and message wordingSettings, starting from defaults that already use their name and their terms.
  • Homework, expressions and the curriculumOff until the tutor turns each one on.
  • A different layoutFree to switch themselves, since their words and photos move with them. A fee only if Dan reworks their pages to suit it.
  • Receipts and tax detailsAsked for when their first card payment arrives, not before.
Building it This is step 5 of the build plan, and it leans on steps 1 to 4.

What it takes

  • The preview is the real siteOnboarding writes a draft tutor record, and the preview renders the real layout from it. What they see is exactly what goes live, so there's no mock-up to keep in step.
  • Layouts that fill themselvesEach of the ten layouts has to look right from any tutor's record: few words or many, one photo, two lessons or six. The look picker needs small live renders that are quick on a phone.
  • DraftingClaude drafts from the pasted text only, in the tutor's voice and the site's language, and marks gaps. The read-through before going live uses the same check.
  • PaymentsStripe Connect with Stripe's embedded onboarding form, which takes our colours, font and corner radius and keeps the tutor on our page. Stripe recommends its newer Accounts v2 for a new platform. Stripe's hosted page is the fallback.
  • GoogleSign-in asks for name and email only. Calendar uses the narrow permission the site already asks for, which only touches calendars the site made itself. Google reviews apps that ask for calendar access before the public can connect without a warning screen, so that review should start early.
  • AddressesA wildcard address and certificate for every tutor's free address on Cloudflare, plus a list of reserved and rude names.
  • BillingA €25 monthly subscription on Stripe Billing with a 30-day trial and no card. The dashboard asks for the card before day 30.
  • InvitesThe WhatsApp invite Inês's site already has, run per tutor.
  • SupportAgents answer tutors' questions and read every new site and every answer to the after-launch question. They pass a case to Dan only when money goes wrong or something looks off.
Your calls Dan agreed both on 9 November 2026. Either can change later without rework.

Two things decided

When does the €25 start?

Decided30 days free from going live, with no card to start.Nothing to hesitate over at the door, and a month is long enough for a first booking.
A card at sign-up, first month free.Fewer tutors start, though more of those who start go on to pay.
Free until their first student pays them.A lovely promise, but tutors paid by hand would never reach it.

Does a new site go live straight away?

DecidedStraight away.A support agent reads each new site within the hour and passes one to Dan only if something looks wrong.
Only after Dan has read it.Safer, but it breaks the twenty-minute promise and makes the best moment wait for Dan.
How we'll know Each step logs how long it took, never what was typed.
